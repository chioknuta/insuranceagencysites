Personal insurance

Coverage for your household

Leon Creek writes the personal side for working families on the West and Northwest Sides of San Antonio: the house off Culebra, the two cars and the teenager's third one, the apartment by the Medical Center, the motorcycle, the manufactured home out toward Lackland, and an umbrella stacked above all of it. Rocío spent nine years on a service desk before she opened this office, so the paperwork is the part we are good at. The ID card for the tax office window, the proof of insurance a lender wants by Friday, the declarations page nobody can find. Bring what you have now, in either language, and we will run it through the carriers we are appointed with and tell you what came back. We answer at (210) 555-0163.

  • Auto

    The auto policy is the piece of paper a family here gets asked for most: at the tax office window when the registration comes due, at the dealer before the keys are handed over, by the finance company for as long as the loan runs. It is written on the cars in one household and everyone who drives them.

    • Rocío quotes it from each car's year, make and model, plus every driver's name, birth date and Texas license number
    • The ID card is printed here at the counter or sent to your phone, whichever gets you to the tax office window first
    • Bring the declarations page you have now. Most of what the carriers ask is already on that one sheet

  • Home

    A homeowners policy is written on the house you live in, the things inside it and your liability as the owner. Around here the first question every carrier asks is how old the roof is, because of the hail, and the second is who holds the mortgage, because the lender's clause has to go on the policy the way the closing papers print it.

    • Rocío starts with the street address. After that it is the build year, the square footage off the appraisal district page, and the roof's age, which the carriers here ask about before anything else
    • Closing on a house in Alamo Ranch or Helotes? Send the closing date and the lender's name from the commitment letter, and the evidence of insurance goes to the title company ahead of the signing
    • After a refinance, the new mortgagee goes on the policy from this desk, typed from the lender's letter

  • Renters

    The leasing office by the Medical Center wants proof of renters insurance before it hands over the keys, usually on move-in day. A renters policy is written on what the tenant owns and on the tenant's liability; the building itself is the landlord's to insure. Most of the ones we write happen on the same call as the kid's first auto policy.

    • Daniela quotes renters in the time it takes to read her the lease address and the move-in date
    • A round number for what the furniture, the clothes and the electronics would cost to replace is all the carrier needs for the contents
    • If the leasing office has to be named on the policy or wants the proof sent to them, Daniela sends it there the same day

  • Motorcycle

    A motorcycle policy is written the way an auto policy is, on the bike and whoever rides it, and the tax office asks for its ID card at the window just the same. Chrome, custom parts and riding gear go on by endorsement, listed piece by piece.

    • Bring the title or the registration, which carries the VIN and the year, make and model, and tell us who rides
    • Say whether it is ridden to work on Loop 410 every day or comes out on weekends, because the carriers rate the two differently
    • For chrome and custom parts the receipts set the value, so bring what you kept

  • Mobile and Manufactured Home

    Mobile and manufactured home policies cover the home, the porch and carport attached to it, and what is inside. They are written on their own forms because of how these homes are built, moved and tied down. Not every carrier writes them, and we know which of ours do.

    • Rocío needs the year, the manufacturer and the serial number off the data plate, and whether the lot under it is owned or rented
    • Tell us whether the home is tied down and skirted, because the carriers ask
    • If the home is financed, the lienholder goes on the policy from this desk, spelled the way the finance company's letter spells it

  • Umbrella

    An umbrella is one more policy stacked above the auto and the home, adding liability limit that only comes into play once the policy underneath has paid its full amount. Before a carrier writes one it wants the auto and home limits raised to a floor it sets, so Rocío quotes the three together.

    • The first thing the carrier reads is the liability limit on the auto and home declarations pages, so have both in hand
    • A teenager on the auto policy, a pool in the backyard or a rent house on the West Side each bring a page of extra questions
    • Rocío prices it alongside the auto and home renewal rather than on its own, so ask when the rest of the account comes up
