Business insurance

Coverage for the business

The commercial side of Leon Creek was built one referral at a time. A tile setter needed a certificate before a job could start, then his cousin's lawn crew called, then the taqueria where they both eat lunch. Today it is small contractors, landscapers and lawn care, food trucks and restaurants, cleaning companies, and the trucks and trailers they all run on Loop 410, I-10 and I-35. Ernesto Pruneda writes that side of the office. Call him at (210) 555-0163 with the trade, the part of town the crews work and the date somebody wants paper by, and he will list what has to be in hand before a quote can go to the carriers.

  • Contractors

    General liability for the small trades: tile, drywall, paint, roofing, concrete, framing, the electrical and plumbing subs, and the handyman with two trucks. It is the policy a general contractor's office asks for before a crew can start on Monday, and certificates go out from this office the same day when the policy allows it.

    • A quote needs the trade, the payroll, the gross receipts, how much work is subcontracted out, and the prior coverage if there was any
    • When the GC wants a certificate, copy the holder's name and mailing address off the contract instead of typing it from memory, and include the job name and whatever additional insured or waiver line the contract carries
    • Builder contracts usually come with their own insurance page. Send the whole page and Ernesto reads it against the policy

  • Landscapers and Lawn Care

    Lawn crews, tree trimmers, irrigation installers and the landscaping companies that keep the yards in Helotes and Alamo Ranch alive through a San Antonio summer. Written as general liability first, with the trailer, the mowers and the trucks handled alongside it.

    • A quote needs the services offered, especially tree work and any pesticide or herbicide application, since carriers rate those separately
    • List the equipment on the trailer with values so it can be scheduled on the policy
    • HOA and property management contracts ask for certificates every season. We keep the holder list on file so renewals go out without a phone call

  • Restaurants and Food Trucks

    Taquerias, food trucks, the panaderia on the corner and the catering trailer that works quinceañeras on Saturdays. Restaurant policies package the property, the liability and the equipment, and a food truck is written as a vehicle and a kitchen at the same time.

    • A quote needs the menu in broad strokes, the hours, the seating, whether alcohol is served, and the square footage or the VIN of the truck
    • The commissary and the lot owner both ask for certificates. Send both names and both go out
    • Spoilage after a power outage and equipment breakdown are written as separate parts of the policy, so a quote lists each one on its own line

  • Cleaning and Janitorial

    Residential cleaners, the janitorial crews working nights in the Medical Center, and the outfits that do post-construction cleanup in the new subdivisions off Loop 1604. General liability first, a janitorial services bond when a client's contract asks for one, and the vans.

    • A quote needs the number of cleaners, the payroll, whether they are employees or contract labor, and the kinds of buildings they clean
    • Keys and alarm codes come up in every janitorial quote, so tell us how they are handled
    • Property managers want the certificate before the first shift. Send the contract's insurance page with the request

  • Business Auto

    The work truck, the van with the ladder rack, the trailer full of mowers and the box truck. A business auto policy is written on the vehicles titled to the company, with the employees who drive them listed on it, and around here most of them never leave Bexar County: Loop 410, Loop 1604 and US 90 out past Lackland.

    • To quote it Ernesto wants every VIN, how far from the yard each truck runs, what rides in the bed or on the trailer, and each driver's name, birth date and Texas license number
    • Hired and non-owned auto is the part of the policy for an employee's own truck on a company errand, and it is quoted on the same form
    • ID cards and certificates for the vehicles come out of this office

  • Workers Compensation

    Workers compensation pays medical care and lost wages for an employee hurt on the job, and the premium comes off the payroll, class code by class code. In Texas most private employers can choose not to carry it, but plenty of general contractors and property managers require it in the contract anyway, so it comes up on almost every commercial account here.

    • The payroll, split by what each person does, is the whole rating basis, so send it that way, with the headcount and the experience modifier if one has ever been assigned
    • Loss runs from the past three years, if the business carried it before
    • Comp takes the carriers longer to price than anything else we write, so Ernesto opens the renewal a month ahead of the date
