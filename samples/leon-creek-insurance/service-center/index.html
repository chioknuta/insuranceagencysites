Service center

Already a customer? Start here.

Selling the policy takes an afternoon. Keeping it useful is the rest of the year, and on Granjero Drive that means paper with a deadline on it: the general contractor on Loop 410 who will not let the tile crew on site until the certificate is in his inbox, the tax office window that closes whether or not the ID card printed, the loan sold to a bank you have never heard of, the truck bought on Bandera Road at lunchtime that needs to be listed before it gets back to the yard. Daniela and Yolanda take those calls at (210) 555-0163, Monday to Friday, 9:00 AM to 6:00 PM, and Saturday, 9:00 AM to 1:00 PM. Each one below is written the way it comes in, the situation first and then what we do about it.

  • Request a certificate of insurance

    The general contractor's office on Loop 410 says no certificate, no tile crew on Monday. Send Ernesto the certificate holder exactly as the contract prints it, name and mailing address, plus the job it belongs to, the additional insured or waiver language the contract calls for, and the deadline the GC gave you. Ernesto reads the insurance page against the policy, and when the policy already carries what the contract asks for, the certificate leaves this office the same day.

    Request a certificate →

  • Add or remove a vehicle

    You are in the finance office at a dealer on I-10 and they want proof before you leave with the car. Read Daniela the VIN off the window sticker, the year, make and model, who financed it, and the day it changed hands, and say whether the old car stays on or comes off. She puts the new one on the policy the same day and sends an ID card that shows the right vehicle, so the dealer has what it needs and so does the tax office when the plates come through.

    Call with the VIN →

  • Add or remove a driver

    The DPS letter came and the sixteen-year-old is licensed as of Tuesday. Or the oldest moved into an apartment by the Medical Center and is buying her own policy. Daniela adds or removes a driver from three things: the name printed on the Texas license, the birthday, and the license number itself, plus the date the change starts. A teenager's report card goes in the file too, because some carriers rate on it.

    Call with the license details →

  • Change a mortgagee or lienholder

    The refinance closed, or a letter says a servicer in Dallas now holds the loan and wants to be on the policy by a date printed at the bottom. Read Rocío the mortgagee clause off that letter, every line of it, plus the loan number, because the lender's system rejects a clause with one word out of place. She endorses the policy with the carrier, sends the new lender its proof of insurance by fax or email, and keeps a copy in your file for the next time the loan changes hands.

    Call with the new clause →

  • Get an auto ID card

    The registration renewal is due, the line at the tax office is out the door, and the card in the glovebox expired last month. Give Yolanda the plate number or the last six of the VIN. She prints a current card at the counter while you wait, or sends one to your phone so you can show it at the window, and if a lender wants to see the vehicle listed she sends the copy straight to them.

    Call for an ID card →

  • Report a claim

    Rear-ended on Loop 410 at five o'clock, hail through the carport in Leon Valley, a water line that let go over Labor Day weekend. The first call is to Daniela, in Spanish or English, whichever comes out when you are shaken. She looks up which of the 21 carriers has the policy, phones the loss in to that company while you wait, and writes the claim number into your file so the office can chase it. After six or on a Sunday, your ID card lists the carrier's own claims number; ring that first, then tell Daniela on Monday so she can follow the adjuster.

    Call (210) 555-0163 →

  • Make a payment

    The bill arrived with a due date and a company name you do not recognize, or a card was declined and the notice gives a cancellation date. Premium never comes through this office; it goes to whichever of the 21 carriers wrote the policy, and every one of them has its own billing line and payment page. Give Yolanda the policy number and she reads you the right one, stays on the phone while you pay if you want her to, and tells you the date the money has to post by so the policy does not cancel.

    Call about billing →
