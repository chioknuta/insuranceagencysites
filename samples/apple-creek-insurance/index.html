Policy service

A policy gets written in an afternoon, and then it gets serviced for a year, which is where the phone comes in. A certificate for a barn going up outside Kidron, a truck bought at the Saturday auction, a refinance, a deer on State Route 3 at dusk in November, which is the month for it. Marcy takes the personal side and Colleen the farm and commercial side, at (330) 555-0179, weekdays from eight until five and Saturday mornings until noon. Below is what to have handy for each, and the one that goes better on paper.

  • Request a certificate of insurance

    Most certificate calls come from a contractor whose customer will not let the crew start until the paper is in the customer's hand, and the crew is usually already in the truck. Colleen issues them from Tannery Street, to you and to whoever asked for it. What she needs is the holder's name and mailing address, letter for letter off the contract or lease, the job it goes with, any wording they want on it, and the deadline the holder set.

  • Add or remove a vehicle

    Most years somebody buys a pickup at the Saturday auction and needs it on the policy before it goes to work Monday. Marcy adds it, or takes off the minivan that sold, or puts the new gooseneck on the farm policy, and she can do it in one call if you have the VIN, the name of the bank holding the note if there is one, and the day the keys changed hands. The year, make and model come off the VIN.

  • Add or remove a driver

    The sixteen-year-old passes the test in the spring, or a son moves to Columbus and takes his car with him, or a hired hand starts driving the farm truck at first cutting. Any of those is a call to Marcy or Colleen with three things off the driver's license, the name, the birth date and the license number, and one thing off the calendar, the day it changes.

  • Change a mortgagee

    Refinances come in a wave whenever rates drop, and the loan getting sold to a bank in another state comes whenever it likes. Either way the lender wants its name on the policy and proof that it is there. Give Marcy the loan number and the new mortgagee clause, spelled the way the lender printed them, and she files the endorsement with the carrier and sends the lender the evidence of insurance it is waiting on.

  • Get an auto ID card

    The glovebox got cleaned out in the spring and the card went with the receipts, which happens. Or the new pickup needs one before the BMV will plate it, or the bank wants proof on the loan. Tell Marcy which vehicle and where to send it. She mails them, or hands them over the counter if you are on the square anyway.

  • Report a claim

    A deer on Route 3, hail on the greenhouse, a fender bent in the lot at the fairgrounds: call the office first. Whoever answers takes down what happened, finds the company the policy sits with, and gets the claim opened before you hang up. After hours or on a Sunday, your carrier's claims line is printed on the ID card and answers all night, and we take it over from there once the office opens.

  • Make a payment

    Premium goes to the company on the policy, never across our counter. Every one of the twenty-nine bills on its own schedule and in its own way, by mail, by draft or online, so when you call, have the policy number in front of you and Marcy or Colleen will say which company and which way.

The agency

Who you are calling

Apple Creek Insurance Agency opened in 1976, when Walt Ruegsegger rented a storefront a half block off the square in Wooster and started writing the farms he had grown up around. He named the office for the creek that comes in from the east side of the county and joins the Killbuck at Wooster, not for the village, though the village has been good to us too. The office has moved once in fifty years, across the street to 128 South Tannery Street, where it is now.

Dale Ruegsegger, Walt's son, took the office over in 1989 and ran it for twenty-six years. He grew the farm book south into Holmes County and added the storefronts and the contractors, and he is the reason the agency carries as many farm appointments as it does. Half the dairies toward Kidron still think of it as his office, which he does not correct.
