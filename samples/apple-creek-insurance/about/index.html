About the agency

An independent agency in Wooster

Apple Creek Insurance Agency opened in 1976, when Walt Ruegsegger rented a storefront a half block off the square in Wooster and started writing the farms he had grown up around. He named the office for the creek that comes in from the east side of the county and joins the Killbuck at Wooster, not for the village, though the village has been good to us too. The office has moved once in fifty years, across the street to 128 South Tannery Street, where it is now.

Dale Ruegsegger, Walt's son, took the office over in 1989 and ran it for twenty-six years. He grew the farm book south into Holmes County and added the storefronts and the contractors, and he is the reason the agency carries as many farm appointments as it does. Half the dairies toward Kidron still think of it as his office, which he does not correct.

Kate Ruegsegger, Dale's daughter, has run the agency since 2015. She grew up in the office, went away to school, came back, and has been licensed since 2006. She is the third generation, and she runs it the way her grandfather did. The same six people answer the phone, a quote goes to several of the 29 carriers we are appointed with, and nobody gets handed to a call center.

Our customers are Wayne County. Dairies, grain farms and produce growers, Amish and Mennonite and otherwise, from Apple Creek and Kidron down to Millersburg. The storefronts on Liberty Street and Market Street. Contractors, trucks and shops. And the houses, the pickups, the boats and the umbrellas of the same families. The office is open Monday through Friday from 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM and Saturday mornings until noon, and the number is (330) 555-0179.

  • Twenty-nine carriers, several that write farms

    The family owns the office; the carriers only appoint it, and there are twenty-nine of them. A dairy or a storefront gets quoted with more than one company, and when a carrier decides it is done with farms, which happens about once a decade, the account moves to another of our companies and the customer keeps the same agency and the same phone number.

  • The farm is a business, and we write it like one

    A dairy is a parlor, a bulk tank, a herd, a feed inventory and a payroll. A grain farm is bins, a dryer and a combine that costs more than the house. Aaron writes it the way the farmer runs it, building by building and machine by machine, and he has stood in most of the barns on his book.

  • Paper, phone and the counter still work here

    A good share of our customers do not do business by email, by choice or by conviction. So we take a quote over the counter, mail the ID cards, and keep a paper file where a paper file is wanted. The website is for the ones who want it.

  • The same six people, the same number

    We do not use a call center. The person who quotes the policy is the person who takes the call about the claim, and the number on the sign on Tannery Street, (330) 555-0179, is the number on the policy.

The people here

  • Kate Ruegsegger

    Owner and Agent

    Kate is the third generation to run the office and has owned it since 2015, when her father handed it over. Licensed in Ohio since 2006, she writes the farm and family accounts that have been with the agency since her grandfather's day, and when a carrier changes its mind about a class of business, the call comes to her. She is at the Wayne County Fair every September, usually in the dairy barn.

  • Dale Ruegsegger

    Agent, second generation

    Dale ran the agency from 1989 to 2015 and built the farm book the office is known for. He is still licensed and still comes in two days a week, and on those days he is on the phone with the dairies he wrote in the eighties, some of which will hold a renewal until he is in the chair, which happens more than Kate would like to admit. If you have been a customer for more than ten years, he probably wrote your first policy.

  • Aaron Zollinger

    Farm and Agribusiness Producer

    Aaron writes the dairies, the grain farms, the produce growers and the hobby farms across Wayne and Holmes counties. He grew up on a dairy outside Kidron, which is why he asks about the bulk tank before he asks about the house. Farm quotes, machinery schedules and the Amish and Mennonite accounts go to him.

  • Greg Fenwick

    Commercial Producer

    Greg writes the storefronts, the restaurants, the contractors, the trucks and the commercial buildings, from the square in Wooster out to Orrville and Ashland. He came to the agency in 2011 from a commercial underwriting desk in Akron, which means he knows what the underwriter is going to ask before the underwriter does.

  • Marcy Thiel

    Personal Lines Service

    Marcy handles the personal side of the service desk: the new car, the new driver, the refinance, the ID cards and the claim call. She came to the office in 2004 and knows most customers by voice.

  • Colleen Sigrist

    Commercial and Farm Service

    Colleen issues the certificates, keeps the machinery and vehicle schedules current, and handles the endorsements on the farm and commercial accounts. If a contractor needs a certificate for a job that starts tomorrow, she is the one who picks up.
