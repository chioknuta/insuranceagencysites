Business insurance

Coverage for the business

Our commercial book starts with the farm and always has. Wayne County milks more cows than any other county in Ohio, and the dairies, the grain operations and the produce growers of Wayne and Holmes counties are most of what Aaron writes. Greg writes what is left, which is more than it sounds: the storefronts and restaurants on Liberty Street and Market Street, the contractors, the trucks and the buildings they park in. Twenty-nine appointments, several with companies that still write farms, means a dairy or a shop is quoted with more than one carrier. Call (330) 555-0179 and say what the operation is and which road it is on, and whoever answers will run through what the carrier is going to want.

  • Farm and Agribusiness

    A farm policy typically puts the house, the barns and outbuildings, the machinery and equipment, the livestock, the stored grain and feed and the farm's liability on one policy. The dairies around Wooster, Apple Creek and Kidron, the grain farms along US 30 and the produce growers who sell at the auction in Mount Hope are what we have written since 1976. A share of those farms are Amish and Mennonite, in Wayne and Holmes counties, and that business comes in by phone and on paper, which suits us. A dairy quote starts with the herd, the parlor and the bulk tank. A grain quote starts with the acres, the bins and the dryer. A produce quote starts with the greenhouses, the high tunnels and where the crop sells.

    • A quote starts with the acreage, what is grown or milked, and every building with its age, construction and use
    • Machinery, equipment and livestock go on a schedule, so a list with year, make and value saves a second call
    • Custom work for neighbors, a roadside stand, hired help or an event on the farm each change the rating, so tell us

  • Main Street Retail and Restaurants

    We write the storefronts on Liberty Street and Market Street, the shops around the square and the restaurants and coffee counters that feed the college and the courthouse. A retail or restaurant policy typically packages the building or the tenant's improvements, the contents and stock, the liability, and for a restaurant the kitchen equipment, spoilage and liquor liability where drinks are served.

    • We start with the building: the address, its age, the square footage, how old the roof is, and whether it is yours or leased
    • For a restaurant, tell us about the hood and suppression system, the seating count, the hours and any alcohol sales
    • Annual sales and payroll are part of the rating, so a rough figure gets a quote moving

  • Contractors

    A contractor's account is usually a general liability policy for the work, an inland marine schedule for the tools and equipment, and a business auto policy for the trucks and trailers. A good number of the crews that put up pole barns, houses and steel buildings in this part of Ohio work out of Holmes County and the south end of Wayne County, and their customers ask them for certificates, additional insured wording and waivers of subrogation. Colleen issues those from the office.

    • A quote starts with the trades performed, the split between residential and commercial work, and payroll and sales
    • Tell us about subcontractors used and whether they carry their own coverage
    • Tools and equipment go on a schedule with make, model and value

  • Business Auto

    Business auto is the policy on the trucks a business holds title to and the people who drive them for it: the milk truck, the grain truck that runs from the first week of harvest until the bins are full, the contractor's pickups and trailers, the bakery's van and the farm's flatbed. Before a carrier quotes it they want to know how far the trucks go, because a truck that stays in Wayne County and one that runs to Pittsburgh are two different rates, and they ask that before they ask about the drivers.

    • A quote needs every VIN and a list of who drives, with birth dates and license numbers
    • Tell us the radius: around the county, into Akron and Canton, or across the state line
    • Colleen issues the certificates and mails the ID cards for the fleet from the same desk

  • Workers Compensation

    Ohio is one of four states where workers compensation is written by the state fund, the Ohio Bureau of Workers' Compensation, and not by private carriers. So we do not sell a workers compensation policy. What we do is help an employer, including a farm with hired help, get set up with BWC, and we write the stop gap employers liability coverage that carriers offer alongside a BWC policy, since the BWC policy does not include it.

    • New employers, farms included, can call and we will walk through where BWC enrollment starts
    • Stop gap employers liability is quoted with the general liability or the farm policy
    • Have the payroll by job type and the BWC policy number if there is one

  • Commercial Property

    Commercial property is the building a business owns or rents and what is in it: the machines, the stock, the counters and coolers a tenant put in. In Wooster that runs from a brick building on the square that has stood longer than the courthouse to a steel shop that went up outside Orrville last year, and the carrier looks at what it is made of, how big it is, the roof, the address and what goes on inside before it puts a number on it.

    • On an older building the carrier asks the age of the roof, the wiring, the plumbing and the furnace, along with the address, the year and the square footage
    • An owned building and rented space go on different forms, so tell us which one this is
    • A statement of values, or the declarations page from the current policy, saves a second call
