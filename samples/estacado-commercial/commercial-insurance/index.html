Business insurance

Coverage for the business

This is the side the office was built on. Module trucks running cotton to the gins in the fall. Grain trucks. Hotshots working the Permian. Farms and ranches from Plainview down to Post. Oilfield service out of Levelland and Brownfield. The gins and ag suppliers that keep all of it moving. We hold 34 appointments, most of them trucking, farm and oilfield paper, not main street packages. Call (806) 555-0128 with the MC number, the unit list or the payroll in front of you. We tell you what the submission needs before you hang up.

  • Trucking

    Module trucks hauling cotton to the gin, grain, hotshot and general freight, running local radius across the South Plains or long haul to the Permian and across the state. We write the auto liability, motor truck cargo, physical damage on the units, non-trucking liability for owner operators leased on, and trailer interchange, and we request the federal filings from the carrier. Trey Muncy has done nothing but trucking since 2009.

    • MC and DOT numbers. Radius. Unit list with VINs and values. Drivers with CDL numbers and birthdates.
    • Three to five years of loss runs. If the authority is active, the filings on it now.
    • Certificates print here. Filing requests leave here for the carrier. Same day if it lands during business hours.

  • Farm and Ranch

    Cotton, grain and cattle, on dryland and under pivots, from the Caprock west to the New Mexico line. The farm policy covers the equipment, the barns, shops and grain storage, the liability that goes with a working operation, and the farm trucks. Lane Dockery grew up on a cotton farm outside Brownfield and writes this line.

    • Equipment schedule: year, make, model, value, every piece. Stripper and module builder included.
    • Every structure with its footprint and what is inside. Acres owned, acres leased.
    • Custom stripping, hay hauling, a neighbor's grain on your truck. Each rates on its own line. Say so up front.

  • Oilfield Service Contractors

    Hotshot, roustabout, water hauling, pump and compressor service, welding, dirt work and lease road maintenance, working the Slaughter and Levelland fields and running down to the Permian. The operator's master service agreement sets the limits and the wording, and we read the MSA before we quote so the certificate matches it.

    • The MSA or the operator's insurance exhibit goes in with the quote request.
    • GL, auto, workers comp and umbrella quote as one package. That is how the MSA lists them.
    • Payroll by class code, the unit list, three years of loss runs. That gets it to market.

  • Cotton Gins and Ag Suppliers

    Gins, seed and chemical dealers, fertilizer, equipment dealers and the elevator. A gin runs hard for ninety days and sits for nine months, and the buildings, the gin stand and press, the modules on the yard, the seed house and the liability are written to match that season. We also write the gin's trucks and the workers compensation for the seasonal crew.

    • Bale count for the last three seasons. Values on buildings and machinery. Payroll for the season.
    • Chemical and fertilizer dealers: product list and how it is stored.
    • Certificates for the co-op, the lender and the module hauler print here.

  • Workers Compensation

    We write workers compensation for the trucking companies, gins, farms and oilfield contractors that carry it, rated on payroll by class code. Drivers, gin crew, farm hands and roustabouts each fall under their own code, and getting the payroll split right is most of the work.

    • Payroll by class code. Last year actual, next year projected.
    • The mod sheet and the loss runs, if the account has been rated before.
    • Comp goes to market slower than anything else in the file. Call early.

  • Business Auto

    The pickups, service trucks and flatbeds of a business that is not a for-hire carrier. The gin's yard trucks, the chemical dealer's delivery units, the oilfield contractor's crew trucks. Rated on the units, the radius and the drivers, and written with hired and non-owned auto where employees drive their own trucks to a lease.

    • VINs, values, radius, driver list. Send all four.
    • Any unit hauling for hire goes on the trucking policy. Flag it.
    • ID cards and certificates print here.

  • Commercial Property

    The shop, the warehouse, the gin buildings, the equipment dealer's lot and everything inside them. Carriers rate it off the address, what it is built of, the square feet and the values on the statement. Out here the roof carries the rate. Hail in May, wind all year.

    • Address, year built, square feet, construction, roof age. Have all five.
    • Statement of values: buildings, machinery, stock.
    • Own the building or lease the space? Different form. Say which.
