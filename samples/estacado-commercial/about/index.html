About the agency

An independent agency in Lubbock

Wendell Brister opened the office in 1987 after eleven years selling tractors, strippers and module builders across the South Plains. He knew the equipment, he knew what it cost, and he knew the people who owned it. The first policies he wrote were the farm policies of the families he had sold tractors to. The trucking followed, because the same families hauled their own cotton to the gin. The oilfield followed the trucking. He still takes the farm and ranch calls from people he has known for forty years.

Today Estacado is an independent agency of seven people in one office on Sandhill Crane Drive, inside South Loop 289 off I-27, appointed with 34 carriers. We write trucking, farm and ranch, oilfield service, cotton gins and ag suppliers, the workers compensation that goes with all of it, and a smaller personal book for the same families. Estacado is the second half of Llano Estacado, the name of the flat country the office sits on.

His daughter Carla Brister finished at Texas Tech in 2000, came into the office the next year, got her license the same year, and took over as principal in 2016. She runs the agency the way it was built. Commercial first, the phone answered by a person, and nobody quotes a truck until they have read the filings. Trey Muncy has run the trucking desk since 2009, Lane Dockery writes farm and ranch, Janice Kimbro manages commercial lines and turns the certificates around, and Marisol Quintanilla and Brenda Kohlmeier handle the service work. Marisol takes the office's calls in Spanish.

The book runs where the roads run. I-27 north to Plainview and Amarillo, US 87 south to Lamesa and the Permian, US 84 southeast off the Caprock through Slaton and Post, west to Levelland and southwest to Brownfield and the oil patch. Cotton is the crop, the South Plains is the largest contiguous cotton-growing region in the world, and from October to December the whole office runs on gin hours. The number is (806) 555-0128, and on a Saturday during harvest it still gets answered if you set it up ahead of time.

  • Commercial first

    The office was built on farm policies and trucking and it still is. Seven people. Brenda runs the personal desk. Everybody else lives in class codes, radius, filings and certificates. The personal book is here for the same families, not the other way around.

  • Read the MSA before the quote

    An oilfield contractor's certificate has to match the operator's master service agreement word for word, and a trucking certificate has to match the shipper's contract. We read the contract first, quote to it, and issue the certificate to match it.

  • Harvest hours

    From October through December the gins run around the clock and the module trucks with them. During those months we open Saturdays by appointment, and a unit that has to be on the policy before Monday can be called in on Saturday.

  • Appointed where the book is

    Thirty-four appointments. No carrier owns this office. Most of the paper is trucking, farm and oilfield markets, not main street packages. The carriers that write cotton haulers are a short list. This office is on it.

The people here

  • Wendell Brister

  • Trey Muncy

    Trucking Producer

    Trey Muncy has written nothing but trucking since he joined the office in 2009. Module haulers, grain, hotshot, oilfield water and general freight. He reads the filings before he reads the application, and he knows which of the 34 carriers will look at a new authority and which will not.

  • Lane Dockery

  • Marisol Quintanilla

  • Brenda Kohlmeier
