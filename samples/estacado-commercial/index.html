Policy service

The policy is the easy part. The work is what comes after. A shipper wants a certificate before the load moves. A unit bought Friday afternoon in Amarillo. A harvest driver starting Monday. A note sold to a new lender. The line is (806) 555-0128, weekdays 8:00 AM to 5:30 PM. October through December, Saturdays by appointment. Below is the list. Tape it to the wall by the dispatch desk.

  • Request a certificate of insurance

    Holder name and address, spelled the way they read on the contract. The contract, lease or MSA behind it. Any wording the holder asks for. The deadline. Janice issues it from this desk during business hours, most of them the same day.

  • Add or remove a vehicle

    Unit in, unit out, or a swap at the dealer. VIN. Year, make, model. Value. Lienholder, if any. Effective date. Trucking unit: owned, or leased on from an owner operator? Say which.

  • Add or remove a driver

    Harvest driver starting, or a driver leaving. Full name as it reads on the license. Birthdate. License number and state. Hire date. Trucking accounts: we pull the MVR first, then the carrier adds him.

  • Change a mortgagee or lienholder

    Refinance on the house. Note sold. New lien on a unit. Forward the lender's clause and loan number, character for character. We endorse the policy and send the lender its evidence.

  • Get an auto ID card

    Lost the one in the glovebox. New unit needs one. County or lender wants proof. Name the unit. Say where it goes. It prints here.

  • Report a claim

    Business hours: this office first. We write it up, name the carrier on that policy and open the file with them. After hours, a unit down outside Post at two in the morning: the claims number is on the ID card. Call it, then call us at 8:00.

  • Make a payment

    Direct bill or agency bill depends on the carrier and the policy. Call with the policy number. Brenda pulls it up and tells you who to pay and how.

The agency

Who you are calling

Wendell Brister opened the office in 1987 after eleven years selling tractors, strippers and module builders across the South Plains. He knew the equipment, he knew what it cost, and he knew the people who owned it. The first policies he wrote were the farm policies of the families he had sold tractors to. The trucking followed, because the same families hauled their own cotton to the gin. The oilfield followed the trucking. He still takes the farm and ranch calls from people he has known for forty years.

Today Estacado is an independent agency of seven people in one office on Sandhill Crane Drive, inside South Loop 289 off I-27, appointed with 34 carriers. We write trucking, farm and ranch, oilfield service, cotton gins and ag suppliers, the workers compensation that goes with all of it, and a smaller personal book for the same families. Estacado is the second half of Llano Estacado, the name of the flat country the office sits on.
